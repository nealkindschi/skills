# ATS Scoring Mathematics

Detailed breakdown of how ATS platforms compute candidate scores. Load when the user asks about scoring mechanics or when producing a scored analysis.

---

## Traditional Deterministic Models

### Skill Match Ratio (40% Weight)

The fundamental calculation for the skill-matching component operates on a straightforward ratio:

```
Score = (number of matched skills) / (total number of skills demanded by the job description)
```

A high score is almost exclusively achieved through verbatim vocabulary alignment. The system heavily rewards candidates who mirror the exact terminology, phrasing, and acronyms from the job posting. Candidates with identical competencies expressed using variant terminology are penalized.

### Standard Weight Distribution

| Dimension | Weight | Evaluation Method |
|---|---|---|
| Skill Match | 40% | Presence, density, and placement of primary and secondary technical/domain-specific skills from JD |
| Experience Level | 30% | Aggregate duration of relevant employment; max points for surpassing chronological thresholds |
| Education Relevance | 20% | Alignment of degree types, institutional prestige, and academic relevance against required qualifications |
| Format and Clarity | 10% | Document length, spelling accuracy, and parsing integrity |

### Granular Point Systems

Some deterministic scoring implementations use highly specific point allocations:

| Factor | Max Points | Full Points Awarded When |
|---|---|---|
| Word Count | 25 | Resume exceeds 300 words |
| Technical Skills | 35 | 8 or more relevant tech skills are explicitly extracted |
| Professional Experience | 40 | Parser calculates 5+ years of tenure |

### Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF)

TF-IDF evaluates specific vectors of parsed resume text. It measures how frequently a term appears in the resume (TF) against how commonly it appears across all resumes in the database (IDF). Terms that are rare across the pool but frequent in the candidate's resume receive higher weights.

### Cosine Similarity

Computes the cosine of the angle between two vectors in multi-dimensional space:
- Vector A: the resume's keyword distribution
- Vector B: the job description's keyword distribution

A cosine approaching 1.0 indicates high alignment. This is the default matching mechanism when no AI/embedding system is present.

## AI and Semantic Vector Embeddings

### Resume2Vec Architecture

Transforms the evaluation process using encoder models (BERT, RoBERTa, DistilBERT) paired with LLM decoders (GPT-4.0, Gemini, Llama).

**Key mechanism**: Both the job description and the entire resume are dynamically mapped as high-dimensional vectors within a shared semantic space. The system calculates mathematical proximity between the job vector and the resume vector to determine relevance.

This eliminates reliance on predefined knowledge bases (like O*NET) and manual feature engineering. The system recognizes underlying intent and skill transferability.

**Example**: An applicant with deep, quantified experience in "data modeling," "Python scripting," and "predictive analytics" is recognized as highly relevant for a "Machine Learning Engineer" role, even if the resume entirely lacks the string "machine learning."

### Accuracy Metrics

| Metric | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (nDCG) | Evaluates the precise accuracy of candidate rankings generated by AI |
| Rank-Biased Overlap (RBO) | Measures consistency between model rankings and actual human hiring preferences |

These systems demonstrate significant improvements in matching accuracy within highly technical domains (engineering, healthcare) by mirroring nuanced human decision-making criteria.

## Scoring Strategy by Vendor Type

### Deterministic Vendors (Taleo, iCIMS, SAP)
- Verbatim keyword matching dominates
- Repeat high-value terms multiple times across the document
- Skills section should list exact JD terms
- Each experience bullet should echo JD language

### AI-First Vendors (Eightfold, Greenhouse)
- Embeddings matter more than exact terms
- Quantified outcomes and specific methodologies carry weight
- Career progression narrative influences vector proximity
- Synonyms and adjacent skills are recognized

### Hybrid Vendors (Workday)
- Skills intelligence bridges deterministic and embedding approaches
- Include both exact matches and adjacent capabilities
- Bullet point quality matters for downstream interview scorecards
